# Free (or nearly) shipping supplies: a reseller's field guide

*Built by Asdale Dynamics, LLC - one of those quiet, practical tools we like to publish. Written the way we explain everything: plain English, step by step, never condescending.*

If you sell on eBay, Mercari, Poshmark, Facebook, Etsy, or out of the trunk of your car, packaging eats your margins in two ways: the stuff you buy (boxes, mailers, tape) and the postage that gets mad at you for choosing the wrong box. This guide is about the first part - and a little about the second - because **the cheapest meter of postage in your life is the one you already pay for.**

Everything here is free, nearly free, or free-with-a-catch. Every price was observed on **2026-11-02** and marked with its date; prices move, especially in January when USPS updates rates. Alongside this guide sits a static HTML directory (`site/index.html`) with the same facts as QR-ready cards, plus a printable bench sheet. No affiliate links appear anywhere in this project - links go straight to the carrier or program.

**How this guide was verified:** every linked page was requested on 2026-11-02 and its HTTP status recorded (see `docs/link-log.md`). A few carrier pages (the USPS Postal Store, for instance) refuse automated requests with `403` even though the pages are public and correct - those are flagged honestly as "bot-shielded" in the link log rather than pretending everything is green.

## The four golden rules

**1. The service owns its branding.**
Free USPS packaging is USPS property and ships USPS Priority Mail/Express. Free FedEx paks ship FedEx expedited. Free UPS express boxes ship UPS express services. Cross-loading a free Flat Rate box with a Ground Advantage label is not "being clever"; per USPS's own FAQ, misuse of supplies **may be a violation of federal law**, and USPS can ding your postage. Same discipline applies to every branded freebie: pickup the packaging, pay for the service that picked the packaging.

**2. Bring your own box for everything else.**
USPS Ground Advantage (GA) - the workhorse lane for sub-16-oz reseller parcels - does not supply free packaging. FedEx Ground and UPS Ground want your box too. That's the honest takeaway: all of your *real* savings come from boxes you did not pay for. Sections below cover stores, reuse, and bulk purchases to get you there.

**3. Packaging money is smaller than postage money - but easier to control.**
A $0.20 mailer versus a $0.55 mailer matters less than choosing a $6.93 tier over a $12.95 one. That said: packaging decisions are made *before* postage decisions. A 12x12x3 box outperforms a 12x12x12 cube for a flat item every time, because dimensional weight and stabilizing weight all flow upstream. Stock **right-sized** packaging even when it's free.

**4. Label hygiene beats luck.**
Remove or black out old shipping labels completely. Reused boxes with visible barcodes confuse scanners and can accidentally route or track wrong; cross-out isn't romantic, it's mandatory. And for USPS-branded boxes you reuse, they must ship the same service again (old labels removed).

### Ordering mechanics

1. Create a free USPS.com account (Click-N-Ship area, or the Postal Store).
2. Order supplies from the **Free Shipping Supplies** listing - packs of 10 or 25 arrive in a few business days, postage-paid.
3. Keep modest quantities in circulation (a couple of packs is plenty) - free deliveries are a checkbox away forever, so no need to hoard.
4. Free carrier pickup: prepaid parcels go out with your regular carrier - the supplies page even notes they ship to you with a "Carrier - Leave if No Response" endorsement.

### The rules, in one breath

- Priority Mail packaging ships Priority Mail. Express packaging ships Express. (USPS store text says Flat Rate supplies "may only be used to ship via Priority Mail Flat Rate"; DMM 115.1.1 says Express packaging "must be used only for Priority Mail Express.")
- Using Priority-branded packaging with FedEx/UPS/Ground Advantage labels = misuse. USPS FAQ: "Misuse may be a violation of federal law." (That also means the paint-it-matte-tan look isn't a loophole - the *branded supply* is what's restricted, so keep branded supplies on their own lane.)
- Reusing USPS-branded boxes is fine **only** for the same service, with old labels removed or fully blacked.
- Don't sell, decorate into art, or hand out USPS supplies as materials.
- USPS supplies contain no dangerous-goods exemptions: lithium batteries, perfume, nail polish, aerosols and candles have their own (mostly Ground) rules. When in doubt, carrier hazmat pages are the only word.

### When Flat Rate wins - worked examples

*Prices below are the observed commercial rates from 2026-11-02.*

- **Dense mini-books to Zone 8:** 4 lb of math textbooks in a **Small Flat Rate box** = $12.95. Four pounds of a weight-based lane (Priority or GA) to a far zone commonly exceeds that. FR wins.
- **A bulky but light sweater:** 8 oz poly-mailed, GA light tier ≈ $6.93 + mailer. A Medium Flat Rate box would be $22.02 for the same sweater. GA wins big. (This is why we scream "right-size": the FR box is free packaging, not free postage.)
- **The padded envelope trick:** the FR **padded** envelope puffs up near clothing size; clothing resellers treat it as their free outer shell, since bulky soft shapes ride at the envelope price.

### Ground Advantage life

- Tiers step at 4 oz / 8 oz / 12 oz / 15.999 oz. Shaving two ounces of paper void-fill can drop you a tier - this is a **real** price lever, not an urban legend.
- Book postage on a platform that auto-includes commercial rates (Click-N-Ship or your label tool; see the Pirate Ship entry in the frugal section).
- Any plain box or mailer works - so pair GA with the store give-aways below and your packaging cost is $0 forever.
- For packing technique itself, eBay's own [labels & packaging tips](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-online/ebayseller-transaction-process?id=4085) page is worth one skim; the short version: wrap each item individually, fill every void, tape every seam.

## Courier programs

### FedEx: One Rate + complimentary expedited packaging

- **One Rate** is FedEx's flat-rate lane. Eligible boxes and envelopes are **free** - order online (needs a free FedEx account) or grab them at a FedEx Office counter. Observed 2026-11-02: the One Rate **envelope** at FedEx 2Day ran **$12.75**. Bigger One Rate boxes cost more but are flat relative to weight.
- Beyond One Rate, FedEx's rule: **complimentary packaging ships with FedEx expedited, FedEx Express Saver, or FedEx International Economy services**; it's front-of-counter free at their stores when you ship that day, and orderable for your door with an account.
- FedEx **Ground** lanes need your own packaging. No freebies there - that gap is where store give-aways earn their keep.

### UPS: account supplies + The UPS Store

- UPS's own words: **"UPS account holders get free UPS shipping supplies including packaging, forms and labels"** - a free profile unlocks the online supplies order; boxes/paks/envelopes arrive in a few business days. The free packaging exists for express lanes (Next Day Air, 2nd Day Air, Worldwide Express); **UPS Ground needs your own box**.
- The UPS Store is a franchise network: supplies are **sold** (prices vary toe-to-toe between stores), their certified packing service is a paid convenience. Useful for one-off odd sizes - golf clubs and chandeliers have no free tier.

### DHL Express: the international freebie

- DHL Express account holders order free packaging through the **Online Supplies Order (OSO)** platform - flyers, envelopes, boxes to 25 kg, tubes; DHL lists most as "No charge applied," with poly flyers expressly free under 100 single-unit orders.
- DHL-branded packaging is not for dangerous goods (DHL's own note).
- For US-domestic resellers DHL is a corner-case; for the occasional international heavyweight who paid for express, it's a beautiful freebie.

## Store give-aways

These cost $0 and beat online prices by the truckload - and every one is "as-observed folk practice." No two stores recycle the same way, so etiquette matters. None of these have a single web page to link; each directory card says "call ahead," which doubles as a nicely human check.

### The source list

1. **Grocery + big-box cases.** Banana boxes (stable, vented, free), egg flats (built-in dividers!), produce and drink cases. Ask the stock manager after the morning restock; offer to take a whole stack. Line banana boxes with a fresh garbage bag; skip wet, greasy, or pest-scarred ones.
2. **Bike shops.** Wheel-and-frame cartons with foam fits. No other free box ships a bicycle. Weekend trade-ins leave the biggest piles - Sunday evening/Monday morning visits win.
3. **Furniture, mattress + appliance stores.** Ask the dock manager (not the showroom) for clean cardboard sheets and foam corner blocks. Flat boards make instant frame/glass protectors; corner blocks turn any plain box into a bumper.
4. **Liquor/wine stores.** The 12-slot dividers are the prize for fragile modules - mugs, ornaments, miniatures. Mind the branding: if the box prints alcohol, ship it inside-out or kraft-covered. USPS prohibits shipping alcohol outright, and carriers treat alcohol-marked packaging as a flag - the plain unprinted boxes are the clean choice.
5. **U-Haul "Take a Box, Leave a Box."** Many neighborhood stores host a free box-exchange zone - leave clean moving leftovers, take castoffs. Not at every location; call first. (Observed 2026-11-02: new boxes from $2.04 if the swap pile is empty.)
6. **Community groups.** Nextdoor free lists, Facebook Marketplace "free," Buy Nothing groups. Search "boxes"/"moving boxes," set saved alerts, respond fast. May and September apartment move-outs are box monsoons.
7. **Office / school copy-paper cases.** Rigid, square, free at any office or print room. The quiet MVP for heavy tools and hardware.

### Etiquette script that actually works

> "Hi! I ship packages, and I could take some of those empty boxes off your hands - saves you the baling." Then: one sentence of thanks, zero drama, and become a name they remember.

And three don'ts: don't arrive unannounced before the store opens, don't take labeled/store-branded boxes you can't explain plain, and don't skip the smell test - bakery and produce boxes love to perfume your collectibles.

## Frugal alternatives

### Level zero: reuse your own inbound packaging

Strike old labels (fully), reuse branded boxes only for their own service (Priority → Priority), reuse plain boxes anywhere. Padded mailers keep their bubble; peel-and-stick a fresh label. Moldy, wet, or smelly = trash, whatever the savings.

### The bulk math (observed 2026-11-02)

| Supply type | Real observed data points |
| --- | --- |
| Poly mailers 10x13 | Staples 100/Box $19.99 ($0.20 ea); Walmart StarBoxes 100 ct $18.40; specialty 1,000-cases ~$55 (≈$0.055 ea); Uline 1,000-case $194 ($0.19 ea) |
| Corrugated boxes 12x12x12 | Uline S-4125 (200# test) $1.16/box at 25 qty; lighter 32-ECT ≈ $1.00/box |
| Packing tape | Dollar Tree clear $1.50 (Jot 400-inch roll $1.25); U-Haul 55-yd $4.95; Amazon single heavy-duty $4.49; 18-roll bulk pack $26.99 (≈$1.50/roll) |
| Bubble/cushion | Dollar Tree 12-in x 6-ft cushion wrap $1.25 (top-up size) |

Two honest lessons hide in that table:

1. **The last zero is worth more than the first dollar.** Mailers at 100-count cost ~3x what 1,000-count costs per piece. If you ship more than ~20 parcels/month, the 1,000-case pays for itself and never loses its value.
2. **Dollar stores are a top-up, not a system.** A $1.25 single bubble mailer is a convenience purchase. A $1.25 *roll of cushion wrap* is excellent - that's the "cheap for odd sizes" lane.

### Newspaper printer roll ends (the free void fill vendor)

Local newspaper and commercial print shops slice **unprinted** newsprint on giant rolls; ask the press/production desk and they'll hand you a few dozen feet free (it's either that or the baler). Clean (no ink! huge for collectibles), fast crumple, recycles fine. One roll ≈ 10-20 boxes of void fill.

### Pirate Ship: free postage platform (the real cost-saver)

Not packaging - but the biggest lever after the boxes themselves. **No monthly fee, no markup, free forever** (their own pitch, observed 2026-11-02): commercial USPS rates, discounted UPS rates, and **Priority Mail Cubic priced below commercial** - the lane that makes tiny dense parcels (books, spare parts, gadgets) dirt cheap. Five minutes to set up; pairs perfectly with your $0 packaging.

> The reseller stack, in one sentence: **free boxes from USPS/stores → right-size to a GA tier or Priority Cubic → book on a free commercial-rate platform → paid-postage trimmings go to tape and mailers you buy in bulk once a quarter.**

## FAQ

**Are free USPS supplies really free?**
Yes - packaging is free. Postage is not (that's the trade). And the packaging is service-branded, so it's a freebie that only unlocks its carrier's lane.

**Can I use a Priority Mail box with a Ground Advantage label?**
No. That's the single most-asked question and the rule remains: USPS-branded supplies go with their corresponding service - worst case, USPS can treat it as misuse (and at best, it doesn't scan).

**Can I paint or sticker my free boxes?**
USPS packaging should stay recognizable as USPS and go with a USPS service - keep it service-honest. Your own plain boxes have no such worries; cover, kraft, or sticker them freely.

**What if my store doesn't give away produce boxes?**
Ask anyway - policy is manager-by-manager. If the answer is no twice, ask the customers service desk; worst case, that corridor of grocery stores is your losing lane and the furniture-dock track is your winning one.

**Is this legal advice?**
No. It's practical pointers with the receipts dated. For hazmat/liquor/anything-restricted, the carrier's own current rules are the only binding text.
